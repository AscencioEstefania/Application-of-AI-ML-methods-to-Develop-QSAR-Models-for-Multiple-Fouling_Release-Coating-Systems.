# Application of AI/ML-methods to Develop QSAR Models for Multiple Fouling Release Coating Systems.
Biofouling represents an environmental and economic problem caused by the accumulation of microorganisms, such as Cellulophaga lytica and Navicula incerta, on submerged surfaces, increasing hydrodynamic resistance and CO₂ emissions. Fouling release (FR) coatings offer a non-toxic alternative by facilitating the removal of these organisms through hydrodynamic forces. This study developed predictive models to estimate the fouling release efficiency of N. incerta at 10 psi and C. lytica at 20 psi using datasets of 75 and 87 data points, respectively. A mix descriptor approach was applied, treating each coating as a mixture system. The key components analyzed included PDMS, PMHS, PEG, and SBMA. The Gradient Boosting models demonstrated strong performance, with R² values of 0.9666 and 0.9026 for training and validation of N. incerta, and 0.8541 and 0.7671 for C. lytica, highlighting key descriptors such as ATSC2se, Xp-4dv, JGI2, and ATSC4dv. The success of the models and combinatorial descriptors underscores the importance of complex relationships between variables and the synergistic effects of the components on fouling release performance.
